IFRS17 TERM LIFE ENGINE
FORMULA & LOGIC PAPER


Starting Note

This project was rewritten many times while learning actuarial modeling,
financial mathematics, and IFRS 17 measurement logic.

The purpose of this document is to explain the mathematical and conceptual structure behind the engine.

The formulas and structures shown here are intentionally simplified for educational clarity.

The project may still contain mistakes or incomplete interpretations.
If you notice any inconsistency, please report it.


===========================================================
1) GENERAL MODEL STRUCTURE
===========================================================

Let:

t  = projection year
i  = policy index
n  = number of policies
T  = total projection horizon

The engine projects every insurance policy separately through time.

Mathematically:

Projection(i,t)

This means:

for every policy i
and every projection year t,

the engine calculates all required insurance cashflows and liability movements.

If:

n = 10,000 policies
T = 20 years

then:

Total Projection Rows
=
n × T
=
200,000 rows

Because of this large calculation volume,
vectorized matrix-style calculations are preferred instead of traditional loops.


===========================================================
2) CORE INSURANCE PROJECTION LOGIC
===========================================================

For every policy-year combination,
the engine calculates:

> Survival Probability
> Mortality Probability
> Lapse Probability
> Active Policy Status
> Premium Cashflows
> Claim Cashflows
> Expense Cashflows
> Net Cashflows
> Discounted Cashflows
> BEL
> Risk Adjustment
> CSM
> OCI movements

These calculations together create the IFRS 17 liability structure.


===========================================================
3) SURVIVAL PROBABILITY
===========================================================

Let:

q_x = mortality probability at age x

Then:

p_x = 1 - q_x

where:

p_x = annual survival probability

At inception:

Survival(0) = 1

because all policies are initially assumed active.

For future years:

Survival(t)
=
Survival(t-1)
×
(1 - q_{x+t-1})

This creates cumulative survival probability through time.


===========================================================
4) MORTALITY PROBABILITY
===========================================================

Mortality probability represents the probability that death occurs during year t.

The formula is:

Death_Probability(t)
=
Survival(t-1)
×
q_{x+t-1}

This adjustment is necessary because death may only occur if the policyholder survives until the beginning of that year.


===========================================================
5) LAPSE PROBABILITY
===========================================================

Lapse probability represents the probability that the policyholder terminates the contract voluntarily.

Simplified form:

Lapse_Probability(t)
=
Persistency(t-1)
×
lapse_rate

Persistency evolves as:

Persistency(t)
=
Persistency(t-1)
×
(1 - lapse_rate)

At inception:

Persistency(0) = 1


===========================================================
6) ACTIVE POLICY STATUS
===========================================================

A policy must both survive and remain in-force.

Therefore:

Active(t)
=
Survival(t)
×
Persistency(t)

This variable is one of the most important projection variables because premiums, expenses, and coverage units depend on active contracts.


===========================================================
7) PREMIUM CASHFLOWS
===========================================================

Expected premium inflows are calculated as:

Premium_CF(t)
=
Premium
×
Active(t)

If premium payment term expires:

Premium_CF(t) = 0

for:

t > premium_payment_term


===========================================================
8) CLAIM CASHFLOWS
===========================================================

Expected claim outflows:

Claim_CF(t)
=
Sum_Assured
×
Death_Probability(t)

General insurance logic:

Expected Claim
=
Benefit Amount
×
Probability of Event

This structure forms the foundation of actuarial valuation models.


===========================================================
9) EXPENSE CASHFLOWS
===========================================================

Expenses are separated into:

> Acquisition Expenses
> Maintenance Expenses

Initial acquisition expense:

Acquisition_CF(0)
=
Acquisition_Cost

Maintenance expenses:

Expense_CF(t)
=
Maintenance_Expense
×
Active(t)

Examples:

> Commission payments
> Operational expenses
> Administration costs
> Claims handling expenses
> IT infrastructure costs


===========================================================
10) NET CASHFLOW STRUCTURE
===========================================================

Net expected cashflows:

Net_CF(t)
=
Premium_CF(t)
-
Claim_CF(t)
-
Expense_CF(t)

Positive values indicate expected profit generation.

Negative values indicate expected losses or heavy insurance outflows.


===========================================================
11) DISCOUNTING LOGIC
===========================================================

IFRS 17 requires fulfilment cashflows to reflect time value of money.

Present value formula:

PV(CF_t)
=
CF_t / (1+r)^t

Where:

CF_t = future cashflow
r    = discount rate

Total discounted value:

PV_Total
=
Σ PV(CF_t)

The engine may use:

> Locked-in discount rates
> Current discount rates
> Yield curves
> Scenario-based curves


===========================================================
12) BEST ESTIMATE LIABILITY (BEL)
===========================================================

BEL represents discounted expected future fulfilment cashflows.

Simplified structure:

BEL
=
PV(Claims + Expenses - Premiums)

Equivalent form:

BEL
=
- PV(Net_CF)

BEL is recalculated at every projection period.


===========================================================
13) RISK ADJUSTMENT (RA)
===========================================================

IFRS 17 requires compensation for non-financial risk uncertainty.

Simplified implementation:

RA
=
CoC_Ratio
×
BEL

Where:

CoC_Ratio
=
Cost of Capital style risk parameter

Real-world approaches may include:

> Confidence Level
> Tail Value at Risk
> Stochastic simulation
> Full Cost of Capital frameworks


===========================================================
14) CONTRACTUAL SERVICE MARGIN (CSM)
===========================================================

CSM represents unearned future profit.

Initial CSM:

CSM_Initial
=
- (BEL + RA)

if:

(BEL + RA) < 0

If contracts are loss-making:

CSM = 0

and a Loss Component may arise.

Under IFRS 17,
future profits cannot be recognized immediately.


===========================================================
15) LOSS COMPONENT
===========================================================

If fulfilment cashflows are unfavorable:

BEL + RA > 0

then:

Loss_Component
=
BEL + RA

This amount is recognized immediately in profit or loss.


===========================================================
16) COVERAGE UNITS
===========================================================

Coverage units determine how CSM is released.

Simplified structure:

Coverage_Unit(t)
=
Sum_Assured
×
Active(t)

Remaining future coverage:

Remaining_Coverage(t)
=
Σ future coverage units

CSM release:

CSM_Release(t)
=
Opening_CSM
×
Coverage_Unit(t)
/
Remaining_Coverage(t)

This creates proportional profit emergence.


===========================================================
17) CSM ROLLFORWARD
===========================================================

CSM evolves through time.

Simplified structure:

Closing_CSM(t)
=
Opening_CSM(t)
+
Interest_Accretion(t)
-
CSM_Release(t)

Interest accretion:

Interest_Accretion(t)
=
Opening_CSM(t)
×
locked_in_rate

IFRS 17 requires locked-in rates for CSM accretion.


===========================================================
18) LIABILITY FOR REMAINING COVERAGE (LRC)
===========================================================

Simplified structure:

LRC
=
BEL
+
RA
+
CSM

LRC decreases as insurance service is provided.


===========================================================
19) LIABILITY FOR INCURRED CLAIMS (LIC)
===========================================================

LIC represents liabilities arising from incurred insured events.

Examples:

> Reported claims
> IBNR claims
> Claims settlement expenses

This project does not model LIC separately in full detail.


===========================================================
20) OCI LOGIC
===========================================================

OCI separates financial market volatility from insurance service result.

Simplified structure:

OCI
=
Current_Rate_Valuation
-
Locked_In_Rate_Valuation

Purpose:

Reducing accounting volatility caused by changing interest rates.


===========================================================
21) GROUPING LOGIC
===========================================================

IFRS 17 requires grouping of contracts.

Typical grouping dimensions:

> Portfolio
> Profitability
> Issue year
> Risk characteristics

Common groups:

> Onerous contracts
> Profitable contracts
> Contracts with low risk of becoming onerous


===========================================================
22) PROJECTION PIPELINE
===========================================================

Main pipeline structure:

config
→ assumptions
→ discount curves
→ policy generation
→ mortality and lapse
→ premium calculations
→ claim calculations
→ expense calculations
→ net cashflows
→ discounting
→ BEL
→ RA
→ CSM
→ grouping
→ OCI
→ outputs


===========================================================
23) MORTALITY TABLE STRUCTURE
===========================================================

The engine supports two mortality structures:

1)
mortality_table.csv

2)
Fallback qx() function

If mortality tables exist,
the engine reads external mortality assumptions.

Otherwise,
mortality is generated dynamically through qx().


===========================================================
24) MATRIX CALCULATION LOGIC
===========================================================

Large-scale actuarial calculations require efficient computation structures.

The engine therefore uses:

> Pandas vectorization
> NumPy calculations
> Matrix transformations

Advantages:

> Faster runtime
> Better scalability
> Cleaner calculations
> Easier rollforward structures


===========================================================
25) VALIDATION & CONTROL MECHANISM
===========================================================

The engine contains validation structures to detect logical inconsistencies.

Examples:

> Negative probabilities
> Survival above 1
> Invalid discount factors
> Missing projection years
> Invalid cashflow balances
> Missing configuration values

In actuarial modeling:

A model running successfully
does not necessarily mean
the model is mathematically correct.
